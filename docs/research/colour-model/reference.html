Same brand colour, four colour models

Every model below builds the same 13-step ladder from the same brand colour. They differ in how they hold that colour's hue as it gets lighter and darker, and whether contrast stays predictable. We chose the hybrid: lightness from CIE L*, hue and vividness from OKLCH.

Worst case

A saturated blue, #0057FF

This is where the models disagree most. At the same step, two models can differ by up to ΔE 11.4, which is plainly visible side by side.

With soft brand colours, the four models are almost indistinguishable: the largest difference at any step is ΔE 1.9, around the edge of what most people notice. The choice matters most for vivid colours.

Why not OKLCH on its own

Can you count steps to get contrast?

colouri.st promises that the distance between two steps tells you their contrast: 7 steps apart meets 4.5:1 for text, in any colour and any pairing. The chart shows the real range of contrast for every pair 7 steps apart, across three test brands and 14 colours.

Method

How this was tested

Three invented brand sets (pastel, saturated, and close hues; 14 colours). Every model uses the same ladder, in CIE L* with a D65 white: 98, 96, 92, 86, 78, 68, 57, 46, 35, 25, 17, 10, 5. Each colour's own hue and vividness are held, and vividness is reduced only where a step would fall outside sRGB. Contrast is WCAG 2, computed from the final hex values. No vividness curve is applied here, so differences come from the model alone.
